# Curriculum Structure and Coverage

Curriculum is a first-class, versioned product foundation. It anchors planning, assessment design, evidence, reporting, and pedagogical action across disciplines.

## Canonical Hierarchy

```text
Course
  -> Unit
    -> Topic
      -> Learning Objective
        -> Indicator or expected competency
```

Institutions may provide frameworks or curriculum packs, but the core model is jurisdiction-neutral. Independent teachers can create their own structure.

## Five Analytical Layers

| Layer | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Planned | What should be taught and evaluated, and when? |
| Taught | What did the teacher confirm was worked, and to what depth? |
| Assessed | What was actually measured, through which criteria/items and with what weight/depth? |
| Demonstrated | What do approved results show, with what evidence sufficiency and confidence? |
| Action | Which gaps exist and what did the teacher decide to do? |

These layers are not interchangeable. An objective appearing in an assessment is not proof that it was taught or achieved.

## Versioned Concepts

- Curriculum framework/version.
- Course curriculum plan.
- Unit, topic, learning objective, indicator/competency.
- Teaching coverage declaration.
- Assessment-objective and criterion-objective alignment.
- Objective achievement snapshot.
- Curriculum gap.
- Pedagogical action.
- Coverage analysis snapshot.

Historical assessment and analysis records retain the curriculum and alignment versions used at that time.

## Alignment Rules

A criterion or closed item may align with one or more objectives. Each alignment records:

- strength or contribution;
- purpose;
- expected cognitive depth;
- direct or indirect coverage;
- author/provenance;
- teacher confirmation state.

AI may propose an alignment. It is not authoritative until accepted under the applicable policy.

## Coverage Rules

- Coverage and achievement are separate.
- Achievement and evidence sufficiency are separate.
- Missing evidence is `insufficient evidence`, never automatically low achievement.
- A single low-weight appearance does not prove sufficient assessment.
- Planned but not taught, taught but not assessed, low achievement, and insufficient evidence are the initial gap types.
- Criteria without objectives and objectives over-represented or without recent measurement are alignment risks.
- Formative and summative evidence remain distinguishable.
- Result corrections create new analysis snapshots; they do not rewrite published historical analysis silently.

## MVP Experience

Primary navigation:

```text
Unit -> Topic -> Learning Objective -> Criteria -> Cohort/Student evidence
```

Summary indicators remain separate:

- percentage worked;
- percentage assessed;
- percentage with sufficient evidence;
- percentage reaching expected achievement;
- critical objectives at risk;
- objectives not yet assessed;
- open pedagogical actions.

An objective detail shows its curriculum description, planned/taught state, assessment alignments, weight/depth, achievement distribution, representative evidence, frequent errors, evolution, learners requiring support, AI proposals, and teacher-adopted actions.

## Migration

Existing subject/topic/outcome strings may be mapped into the structured model and retained temporarily for compatibility. They are not the target analytical source of truth. Migration must preserve original text and provenance and flag ambiguous mappings for teacher review.
